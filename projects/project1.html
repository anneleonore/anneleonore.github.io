About this study

I examined whether women and men are described differently in workplace feedback. This page shows one study where I used natural language processing (NLP) to analyse developmental feedback entries managers submitted through Culture Amp’s platform.

Methods
  • Agentic and communal language: A language model (BERT) turned each mention of the employee into a numerical representation of how they were described in that sentence (a contextual embedding), and measured how close it was to the same kind of representation for agentic traits (such as “ambitious”) and communal traits (such as “supportive”).
  • Distinctive words: Identified which adjectives appeared more often in feedback to women compared to men, using a statistical method that accounts for how common each word is (weighted log-odds).
  • Sentiment: Classified how positive or negative each sentence was, using a language model (RoBERTa) that agreed with human coders 90% of the time.
  • Hedging: Detected uncertain wording, such as “maybe” or “I think”, using a language model trained to recognise hedges (HedgeHog).
  • Statistics: Multilevel models to compare feedback by employee and manager gender, and longitudinal models to track changes across review cycles.
